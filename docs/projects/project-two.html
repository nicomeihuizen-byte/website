One Acre, Zero Dependency

An off-grid, AI-driven homestead project across two plots one farmed through summer, one through winter migrating with the seasons.

// overview

AI-driven off-grid farming and homesteading

This project follows two plots across the seasons: one farmed through summer and one through winter, with the hardware, code, training data, and failures documented in public through 2026/2027.

Why this project exists. Most "smart farming" projects assume grid power, cloud connectivity, and a budget for commercial sensor kits. This one starts from the opposite constraints: no grid, patchy connectivity, and hardware built from parts that fit in a campervan. The goal is to answer a simple question: how much of a small farm's daily decision-making can genuinely off-grid AI handle end to end, from sensor to scheduler?

Raspberry Pi soil and weather sensor stations

Raspberry Pi-powered weather and soil stations are housed in a solar-powered birdbox on a stake. Wired ground sensors measure moisture, temperature, and nutrients, feeding readings back over a low-power local link rather than relying on cloud connectivity.

LSTM solar forecasting and battery health prediction

Time-series forecasting models use LSTM techniques to track solar production and predict battery health, so the system can plan power-hungry tasks, such as running the vision model, around actual solar availability instead of a fixed schedule.

LLM planting scheduler for biodynamic farming

An LLM-driven planting scheduler combines the biodynamic calendar with global soil and weather datasets, turning sensor readings and calendar positions into concrete planting and watering recommendations.

Compost, fertilizer, and food inventory tracking

Anomaly detection supports compost and fertilizer timing, while a lightweight vision and recommendation model tracks food inventory. The system is designed around clear states and useful feedback as the data grows.

Status and what's next

The project is being built and documented in public through 2026 and into 2027, plot by plot and season by season. Planned next steps include closing the loop between the LSTM forecasts and the LLM scheduler, and publishing the training data as each season completes.
