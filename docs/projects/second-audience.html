Second Audience

Your website has two kinds of visitor now. One has eyes. The other is agentic AI: it never runs your JavaScript, reads only what your server sends, and then answers questions about you for its visitors, who never open your page. Second Audience measures what that second visitor can actually do.

Since August 2026 the scan also measures what four to five agents make of the gap in practice, in fresh sessions, under five rules that keep the agent's answer from ever becoming the finding itself.

// overview

What this measures

An agent such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Perplexity fetches a page, skips the JavaScript, and answers questions about a business in its own words. Second Audience measures what that agent can actually read, what it can actually do, and then names the smallest change that fixes the gap.

Five layers, one verdict per task

Rendering: the page fetched with and without JavaScript, compared. Readability: where a fact actually lives, in text, in markup, in an image, or nowhere. Permission: the intent of robots.txt against its measured effect. Usability: whether an agent finishes the task a visitor came to do, and whose page the answer came from. Proof: thirty to ninety days of classified server log, a layer we advise against more often than for. Every task gets a named verdict instead of a score out of a hundred, because a score invites gaming the very thing it claims to measure.

Seven steps, and the agents come last

Identify the object that actually carries the revenue, extract the facts a buyer needs before committing, hunt for a second version of the same fact worded differently elsewhere on the domain, check whether it survives as text or only as an image or an escape word like "from", map what third parties already say about it, test the finding against published regulator rulings, and only then ask the agents, with the prediction written down first.

What a finding looks like

A private bank's page title claimed "since 1804"; its own published timeline said 1881. A museum's most expensive room said only "from €299"; nine agent answers produced five different prices, and not one gave the weekend rate the room is actually booked on. A regulated manager never published its minimum investment; five agents supplied a figure anyway, unprompted.

Five rules for the agent layer

The agent's answer is never the finding, only ever illustration beside a fetchable piece of text on the page. Every claim gets the same follow-up: at exactly which URL does that appear? Sessions start fresh with the prediction written down first, our own model sits in the same table with no exemption, and our own wrong predictions are published alongside the hits.

Status

Seven scans completed as of August 2026: a museum, an expert network, a regulated asset manager, a private bank, a global brewer, a retail bank, and a coding bootcamp. The server-log layer exists but is recommended against more often than for.
