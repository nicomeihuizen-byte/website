Five

Five deals a week. You will know when one goes quiet. Deal management for one to five sellers, built by someone who carried a bag for fourteen years.

// overview

You already own a CRM

Be honest about the last time anyone filled it in. This is not that. There are five slots, because five is a week's work. You write a line after each call. It reads the history and tells you which deal has stopped moving, and why.

What it actually says

"Both contacts gave a positive buying signal and want implementation, but the deal is blocked on an administrative gap: no entity, VAT or company number, so the local office cannot raise a purchase order. There is a plan, a rep flying out on the 26th, but that is over three weeks away with no interim step."

That is not a summary of your notes. That is a read on the deal, which is what a good sales manager does in a pipeline review. Most teams this size have never had one.

Three things, and no more

Pick five. Write what happened. Get told what is dying. The cap is the feature: a list of everything is a filing cabinet, and five is a week's work.

Built by someone who carried a bag

Fourteen years selling enterprise software at IBM, Oracle and Minitab, then building the tool I wished I had had. The same codebase runs the public demo and the copy I actually sell with, which is the more honest test of the two.

Under it

Next.js 16 and Postgres, with row level security so a tenant's data is isolated at the database rather than by a filter someone can forget. Claude reasons over your own note history and never over anyone else's. Deployed as your own instance on European infrastructure in Finland, so customer notes never leave the EU. Light and dark as standard, your brand on request.
